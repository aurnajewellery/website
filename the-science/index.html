What a laboratory-grown diamond is

A laboratory-grown diamond is diamond: crystallised carbon with the same chemical, physical and optical properties as a diamond mined from the earth. The difference is origin. It is grown in a controlled process over weeks, rather than formed underground over a very long period.

Because the material is the same, a laboratory-grown diamond can't be told apart from a mined diamond by eye or with a standard jeweller's loupe. Specialist laboratory instruments are used to confirm origin, which is one reason independent grading reports matter.

In India, the standard IS 19469:2025 requires these stones to be described as "laboratory-grown diamond" or "laboratory-created diamond". AURNA uses that wording on every label, specification and document.

  • Carbon

    The same element and cubic crystal structure as a mined diamond.

  • 10

    Hardness on the Mohs scale, the same as a mined diamond.

  • 2.42

    Refractive index, which governs how the stone bends and returns light.

  • Graded

    On the same 4Cs as a mined diamond, by independent laboratories.

How they are made: HPHT and CVD

There are two growth methods. Both start with a thin slice of diamond, called a seed, and add carbon to it atom by atom.

HPHT: high pressure, high temperature

A press recreates the conditions deep in the earth where diamonds form: very high pressure and temperatures above 1,300 °C. Carbon dissolves in molten metal and crystallises onto the diamond seed.

CVD: chemical vapour deposition

A carbon-rich gas, usually methane with hydrogen, is energised into a plasma inside a low-pressure chamber. Carbon atoms settle onto diamond seed plates and build up the crystal in layers. Some CVD stones are treated after growth to improve colour; treatments are disclosed where required.

Origin and quality are separate questions

Origin tells you where a diamond came from: a laboratory or the ground. Quality tells you how well it is cut, how colourless and clear it is, and how much it weighs. A laboratory-grown diamond can be of any quality, just like a mined one, so each stone is graded on its own merits.

The 4Cs

Cut, colour, clarity and carat weight describe a diamond's appearance and character. They are graded the same way for laboratory-grown and mined diamonds.

  • Cut

    How well the stone's proportions, symmetry and polish return light. It has the largest effect on brilliance.

  • Colour

    Graded on a scale from D (colourless) to Z (light yellow or brown).

  • Clarity

    The presence of tiny internal or surface features, graded from Flawless to Included.

  • Carat

    Weight, not size. One carat is 0.2 grams.

IGI certificates

The International Gemological Institute (IGI) is an independent laboratory that grades diamonds. An IGI report for a laboratory-grown diamond records:

  • that the stone is laboratory-grown, and the growth method where it is identified
  • its shape, measurements and carat weight
  • its cut, colour and clarity grades
  • any post-growth treatment
  • a unique report number, which is often laser-inscribed on the stone's edge (girdle) so the stone can be matched to its report

Report details can be checked on IGI's website using the report number.

BIS hallmarking on gold

Hallmarking by the Bureau of Indian Standards (BIS) is mandatory for gold jewellery in the districts BIS has notified, which now cover most of India. The hallmark has three parts:

  • the BIS standard mark
  • the purity, in karat and fineness
  • a six-character HUID (Hallmark Unique Identification) code, unique to that piece

Customers can check a HUID with the BIS CARE app. AURNA gold is supplied in three purities:

9K37514K58518K750

Platinum and silver purity is stated as documented for each piece.

What comes with each piece

Every piece comes with the documents that support what you tell your customer: an IGI certificate for the laboratory-grown diamond and a BIS hallmark with HUID on gold.
